The Scale of the Problem

Amsterdam is known as a bicycle city, but it has a downside — a rising number of abandoned bikes taking up space in the city. With 1.5 bikes per resident, there’s an estimated 15% of outdoor bikes which remain unused and block sidewalks and parking spaces.

The city’s problems with bike parking are infamous. Of the 800,000 bicycles in the city, more than 200,000 are abandoned at a given time, hindering pedestrians and cyclists alike.

Parking Congestion

Reduces available spaces for active cyclists by up to 25% in central areas

Pedestrian Access

Blocks sidewalks and creates obstacles for people with mobility challenges

Degrades the aesthetic appeal of our beautiful city streets

How Amsterdam Responds

1

Identification

The city places a clear zip tie around the frame and spokes of potentially abandoned bikes. This fragile marker breaks if the bike is moved.

2

Verification

After 3 months, inspectors return. Bikes with intact zip ties receive a removal notice, giving owners one final month to claim them.

3

Collection

Unclaimed bikes are taken to the central Fietsdepot (Bicycle Depot), where they're held for six weeks before being processed for reuse.

The Bicycle Depot Challenge

Amsterdam's storage facility in the harbor area has space for 75,000 bikes annually, but now collects nearly 100,000. In 2023 alone, 48,000 bikes went unclaimed, with 75% needing significant repair.

This overwhelming volume has led to innovative solutions for giving these bikes new life.

Giving Bikes a Second Chance

From Amsterdam to Jordan: Bikes for Refugees

In 2024, Amsterdam began an innovative program shipping unclaimed bikes to refugee camps in Jordan. The first shipment of 350 bikes is paving the way for a total of 1,400 bikes to be sent after repairs by volunteers.

These bikes serve multiple purposes in the camps:

  • Transportation for refugees moving within the camps
  • Converted to rickshaws for moving people and goods
  • Adapted into cargo bikes for small business ventures
  • Providing economic opportunities through repair workshops
"What was once urban waste in Amsterdam becomes vital transportation and economic opportunity in refugee communities."
