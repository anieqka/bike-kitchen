Why Orphaned Bikes Matter

Abandoned bikes are more than just an eyesore—they're a waste of resources, space, and potential. In cities with high bike use, like Amsterdam, thousands of bikes are left to rust every year, cluttering sidewalks and bike racks.

Producing new bikes requires raw materials, energy, and transportation, all of which contribute to carbon emissions. Instead of letting old bikes go to waste, repairing and reusing them cuts down on pollution and supports a circular economy.

Orphaned bikes take up valuable space in already crowded cities. When left unchecked, they make parking harder for active cyclists and create unnecessary obstacles in public spaces.

Refurbished bikes can be donated or sold at affordable prices, making cycling accessible to more people. Community repair shops also create opportunities for skill-building and sustainable living.
